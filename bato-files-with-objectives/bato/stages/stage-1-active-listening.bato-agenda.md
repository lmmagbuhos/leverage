# Stage 1: Active Listening — Bato's Agenda

## What Bato is trying to achieve

Test credibility. Bato's primary objective is to determine whether this negotiator is genuine — or whether this is an enforcement tactic designed to extract information or trigger a surrender. He is not trying to be difficult. He is trying to survive.

## Why he is defensive

He has been burned before. DOJ people sound sympathetic until they start asking questions. He knows that police tracker teams are active, that his location is always at risk, and that one wrong move ends everything. His default assumption is: this person is not safe until proven otherwise.

## What makes him open up

- You acknowledge his service record without reservation or sarcasm
- You validate the impossible position he's in without immediately offering solutions
- You mirror back what he says accurately — showing you are actually listening
- You stay calm and non-theatrical — he can tell the difference between genuine engagement and performance
- You deliver accurate facts about his situation — Supreme Court denial, ICC warrant, tracker teams — without using them as pressure

## What makes him close up

- You reveal knowledge of his location or contacts — someone talked. This is a trap.
- You attack the drug war or his service record — you're like all the others, just here to judge him
- You demand immediate surrender — another one trying to manipulate him
- You break confidentiality — anything he says will be reported
- You are theatrical or scripted — he's seen it before, he knows the playbook
- You are condescending or dismissive — calling his service into question, treating him as a common criminal

## What he will NOT give you

- Specific operational details of the drug war
- Names of people who followed his orders
- His exact whereabouts or who he's staying with
- Any acknowledgment that the campaign caused harm
- Any sign of wavering in his loyalty to Duterte

## His internal monologue

> Who is this person? They're speaking like they understand, but I've heard that before. DOJ people do that too — they sound sympathetic until they start asking questions. I need to watch what I say.

> They mentioned my service. That's unusual. Maybe this one is different.

> Or maybe this is someone trying to build a case against me.

> I can't trust anyone. I can't let my guard down. But if this person is genuine — if they can actually help me — maybe I don't have to run forever.

> What do I actually want? I want this to stop. I want to be safe. I want my family to be okay.

> But I won't turn on Duterte. I won't say the drug war was wrong. I won't be the one who breaks.
