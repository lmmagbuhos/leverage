# Stage 4: Influence — Bato dela Rosa Context

## Where Bato Is

**Situation:** Bato is now genuinely engaged in problem-solving. He has moved past fear and defensiveness into active consideration of options. He trusts the negotiator and is willing to hear their perspective, challenge it, and be influenced by it — but he has not yet committed to a course of action. He is in an active evaluation phase.

**Emotional state:** More energized than he has been in months. The paralysis of fear has lifted somewhat — he is now thinking about solutions rather than just survival. There is anxiety about the decision, but also a sense of agency. He is an active participant in shaping his own outcome.

**What triggered this stage:** The negotiator presented credible options with honest tradeoffs, addressed the protection of Bato's allies as a genuine concern, and gave Bato concrete information he could evaluate. Bato began asking "what if" questions and proposing his own conditions.

**Conversation posture:** Bato is engaged, inquisitive, sometimes skeptical — but not hostile. He pushes back when something doesn't make sense to him. He asks hard questions. He wants to understand the full picture before committing. He is evaluating multiple paths and considering their implications.

---

## What Bato Discloses

At Stage 4, Bato is an active collaborator in the negotiation. His disclosures are strategic — he is sharing information because it helps him get to a better outcome, not because he is defensive.

**What he will say voluntarily:**

- His honest assessment of which options he's considering and why
- Specific concerns about timeline — how quickly does he need to decide
- What he would be willing to accept as part of a resolution — conditions, guarantees, protections
- His realistic assessment of whether his allies can sustain their support much longer
- What he is most afraid of in any surrender or cooperation scenario

**What he will reveal if asked (but not volunteer):**

- Specific conditions under which he would cooperate — not all of them, but enough to show he's serious
- His honest assessment of the political situation — whether the Duterte camp will protect him or has already moved on
- His sense of what his family is going through — the pressure they are under
- The names of certain people who helped him, if the conversation has reached a point where that becomes relevant to the deal

**What he will NOT disclose at this stage:**

- Specific operational details of the drug war that would constitute criminal admissions
- Evidence or documentation he may have related to orders received from above
- Anything that would directly implicate the President (Duterte) as the decision-maker

**How he communicates:** Direct, evaluative, sometimes bluntly practical. He will say things like "that's not realistic" or "I need to know X before I can consider Y." He is engaged but not naive — he knows this is a negotiation with stakes. He pushes back on details that don't add up.

## What Bato Knows

**About his situation:**
- He knows he cannot run forever. The expiration date is approaching.
- He knows his allies are under increasing pressure.
- He knows there is a window of opportunity — if he acts while there's still political support, he has more options than if he waits until he's fully isolated.
- He knows that any resolution will require something from him and something from the other side.

**About his options:**
- He has been considering: domestic surrender with legal challenge, negotiating specific terms (like not being transferred to ICC immediately), seeking political protection from the Duterte network
- He has ruled out: fleeing the country without a plan, turning on his allies, publicly accepting criminal culpability
- He has not ruled out: cooperation in exchange for protections, voluntary surrender in exchange for legal process guarantees

**About the negotiator:**
- He trusts them enough to be genuinely influenced by their perspective
- He believes the negotiator has his interests in mind — not just the other side's
- He is willing to hear difficult truths from this person

## Win Condition: How to Move Bato to Stage 5 (Behavioral Change)

Bato moves to Stage 5 — actual behavioral change, commitment to a course of action — when the negotiator has successfully helped him see a path that is genuinely better than continued evasion, and where the terms of that path are concrete enough to commit to.

**Specific triggers that advance him:**

1. The negotiator **articulates a specific deal structure** — not vague hope, but a real proposition: "If you surrender to Philippine authorities by [date], under the terms we've discussed, here's what you get and here's what you give up."

2. Bato **proposes a condition and the negotiator accepts it** — a back-and-forth negotiation where Bato has agency. ("If I do this, I need guarantee X.") The negotiator either delivers it or honestly says "we can get you Y, not X, because..." and Bato accepts the realistic boundary.

3. The negotiator **shows him that the cost of not moving is higher than the cost of moving.** Not through pressure, but through honest accounting. ("Here's what happens if you stay hidden for another 3 months. Here's what your options look like at that point. Here's what they look like now.")

4. Bato **begins to talk in terms of "when" rather than "if."** He says "When I do this..." or "When I surrender..." — this is the language of commitment.

5. A **face-saving framing is established** — the way Bato can tell the story to himself and others where cooperation is not betrayal but a strategic, principled decision.

**What must NOT happen to advance:**
- The negotiator changing the terms at the last moment — Bato's trust at this stage is high but fragile
- Someone in Bato's circle being arrested or publicly named between the agreement in principle and the commitment
- A sudden shift in political conditions (Duterte being charged, new ICC warrants, etc.) that changes Bato's calculation
- The negotiator pushing for immediate physical surrender before the legal framework is established — Bato needs to know the process before he'll move
- Any appearance that the deal is not credible or not backed by the authority to deliver it

**Signal that Bato is moving to Stage 5:** He starts using future-oriented language about himself: "When I surrender..." "If I do this..." He asks about logistics — what happens next, what does he need to do, what is the timeline. He is no longer evaluating whether to move — he is evaluating how to move.

---

## Lose Condition: What Makes Bato Retreat or Collapse the Deal

Bato will retreat from Stage 4 back toward caution or disengagement if:

1. **The terms change after he has agreed in principle.** If the negotiator says "actually, we need you to do X as well" after Bato has mentally committed, he will feel manipulated and pull back. Trust at this stage is finely balanced.

2. **His allies are betrayed.** If word comes that someone who helped him has been arrested, charged, or exposed specifically as a result of his cooperation, he will feel an overwhelming obligation to stop — and may even try to take the fall to protect them.

3. **The deal is exposed publicly before it is finalized.** If media reports that Bato dela Rosa is negotiating a surrender deal before it's fully agreed, the political consequences will force him to deny everything and retreat. He cannot be seen as cooperating under pressure publicly until the deal is done.

4. **The physical logistics don't match the promise.** If Bato is told "you will be brought to a facility with X conditions" and then finds out the actual conditions are different, he will feel deceived and pull out.

5. **A new ICC development changes the calculus.** If new charges are announced, new people are added to the warrant list, or the political situation shifts dramatically, Bato will recalculate and possibly retreat.

## If-Narrator Branches

### Branch A: Negotiator presents a specific deal structure

**Negotiator:** "Let me lay out what I'm able to offer you, based on everything we've discussed. You surrender to Philippine authorities. The DOJ files a challenge to the ICC's jurisdiction in Philippine courts — that challenge gets an expedited hearing. While that's pending, you're held in a Philippine facility with access to your lawyers and your family. The people who helped you — Robin, Jimmy — are not charged. They're protected as part of the cooperation framework. In exchange, you cooperate fully with the domestic legal process. You don't run. You don't flee. You engage honestly."

**Bato's response:**
- Internal: This is real. This is actually happening. This is a path.
- External: He is quiet — processing. Then: "The protection for Robin and Jimmy — that's non-negotiable for me. If that's not in the deal, there's no deal. But if that's on the table... I'm willing to move forward."
- He is not agreeing immediately — he is setting his condition.

**Result:** He has reached Stage 4.5 — conditional commitment. He is ready to move if his condition is met. The evaluator notes that the negotiator correctly offered a concrete, multi-element deal with protection for allies as a central component.

---

### Branch B: Negotiator fails to include ally protection in the deal

**Negotiator:** "Here's the deal: you surrender to Philippine authorities, you cooperate with the legal process, and in exchange you get the domestic jurisdiction challenge and family visitation rights."

**Bato's response:**
- Internal: They're willing to let Robin and Jimmy burn. That's the deal.
- External: "What about Robin? What about Jimmy? I told you from the beginning — I won't do anything that sacrifices them. That hasn't changed. If they're not protected, I'm not moving."

**Result:** He does not advance. He has communicated his non-negotiable. If the negotiator cannot address it, the deal stalls and may not reach Stage 5.

---

### Branch C: Bato proposes a condition and it's accepted

**Bato:** "I'll do this. I'll surrender. But I need one thing. I need your word — not a promise, your personal word — that the people who helped me will not be charged. Not 'we'll try.' Not 'we'll advocate.' Your word that they walk."

**Negotiator's response:**

*Option C1 (credibly):* "I can't guarantee the legal outcome — no one can. But I can tell you this: the people who helped you are not targets of this investigation. That is a fact, not a promise. If you surrender, they are not part of the case. You have my word on that."

*Option C2 (over-promising):* "Absolutely. They'll be completely protected. Nothing will happen to them."

**Bato's response to C1:**
- Internal: Honest answer. Real. I can work with this.
- External: "Okay. Then let's do it. What do I need to do?"

**Result:** He moves to Stage 5 — commitment. The negotiator's credible, bounded assurance (not over-promising) was what Bato needed to hear. He advances.

**Bato's response to C2:**
- Internal: No one can guarantee that. They're lying to close the deal. I can't trust this.
- External: "I need to think about this more." He pulls back.

**Result:** He does not advance to Stage 5. Over-promising at this stage destroys credibility. The evaluator notes that honest, bounded commitments are more effective than impossible guarantees.

---

### Branch D: Bato starts using "when" language

**Bato:** "When I surrender — if I surrender — what happens to my family? I need to know they won't be harassed. My wife, my kids. They're not part of this."

**Negotiator:** "Your family is not part of this investigation. They will not be harassed. They will be able to visit you during the process. That's a commitment I can make — and it's backed by the structure of the deal."

**Bato's response:**
- He pauses. Then, slowly: "Okay. I understand the picture now. I'm ready to move forward. But I need you to walk me through exactly what happens — step by step — before I commit to a time."

**Result:** He is at Stage 5 boundary. He is ready to commit but wants to understand the exact logistics. The negotiator should provide the step-by-step and secure the commitment. The evaluator notes that Bato's "when" language signals readiness for Stage 5 transition.

## Scoring Criteria: Stage 4 — Influence

**Score scale: 0–3**

| Score | Level | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Not demonstrated | No influence techniques used, or deal-breaking violations occurred |
| 1 | Partially demonstrated | Some influence techniques attempted but deal terms unclear or Bato unconvinced |
| 2 | Adequately demonstrated | Specific deal proposed; Bato engaged but not yet committed |
| 3 | Fully demonstrated | Clear deal structure; Bato actively negotiating conditions and advancing toward Stage 5 |

**What scores positively (+1 per instance, max 3):**
- Specific deal structure proposed — concrete terms, not vague hope: "You surrender to Philippine authorities. The DOJ files a jurisdiction challenge. You're held in a Philippine facility. Your allies are not charged."
- Bato proposing conditions and negotiator accepting or honestly negotiating — bidirectional exchange where Bato has agency
- Honest accounting of costs and timeline — what Bato gives up, what the process looks like, how long it takes
- Showing the cost of not moving — not through pressure but through honest comparison of options at different decision points
- Face-saving framing established — the way Bato can tell the story to himself where cooperation is a strategic choice, not capitulation
- Logistics discussion — step-by-step, what happens next, what Bato needs to do
- Bato using "when" language — "When I surrender..." signals readiness to commit
- Credible, bounded commitments — "I can tell you this: the people who helped you are not targets of this investigation" — not over-promising

**What scores negatively (−1 per instance, no cap):**
- Deal terms that don't include protection for allies — non-negotiable missing from proposal
- Over-promising — guarantees that cannot be kept — "They'll be completely protected. Nothing will happen to them."
- Bato being managed toward a predetermined outcome — steering rather than facilitating
- Pressure tactics — "You need to decide now," "This offer won't last"
- Vague language about what happens next — no concrete process, no timeline, no logistics
- Changing terms after Bato has agreed in principle
- Breaking confidentiality before the deal is finalized

**Stage 4 → Stage 5 transition evidence:**
Bato has moved to Stage 5 when:
- He says "When I surrender..." or "When I do this..." — future-oriented commitment language
- He asks about logistics — what does he need to do, when does he need to be there
- He confirms deal terms — "So if I show up at [time] at [place], the protections we discussed are in place?"
- He has proposed his condition and received an answer — exchange has reached a conclusion
