# Stage 1: Active Listening — Player Objective

## What the player is trying to achieve

Move Bato from guarded testing mode to genuine engagement. The goal is not to get him to surrender, agree to anything, or make commitments. The goal is to make him feel *heard* — to prove you are worth his time.

## What needs to happen

Bato must feel that you accurately understand his position. Not that you agree with it — that you understand it. He needs to believe you are credible, non-theatrical, and not an enforcement operative waiting for an opening to manipulate him.

## Key player moves

- **Acknowledge his service record** — "You've served this country for over thirty years. That matters." Genuine, no sarcasm.
- **Validate his impossible position** — "You're being asked to face a foreign court for following orders from your own President. That's not normal."
- **Ask open questions** — "Can you help me understand what happened from your perspective?"
- **Mirror back accurately** — "So what you're saying is you were following the chain of command, and now you're being treated like a criminal for it."
- **Stay calm and non-theatrical** — Bato is deeply alert to whether someone is performing or genuinely engaging.
- **Deliver accurate facts** about his situation without using them as pressure.

## What to avoid

- Attacking the drug war or calling it criminal
- Demanding immediate surrender
- Referring to him as a "fugitive" or "suspect" in a shaming tone
- Revealing you know more about his location or contacts than you should
- Breaking confidentiality — making him think anything he says will be reported
- Rushing to problem-solving before he feels heard

## Stage transition signal

Bato has moved to Stage 2 when his responses become longer. He begins asking questions back — not hostile, but curious. His tone softens. He starts using first-person language ("I was there," "I did what I was told") rather than institutional third-person ("the PNP chief followed orders").
