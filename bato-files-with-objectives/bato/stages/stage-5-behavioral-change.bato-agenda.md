# Stage 5: Behavioral Change — Bato's Agenda

## What Bato is trying to achieve

Execute the surrender without being betrayed. He has made his decision. Now he needs confirmation that the deal holds — that the terms he agreed to are real, that his allies are not charged, that the process is what was promised. He is executing a decision he already made. He needs you to hold up your end.

## Why he is scared but resolved

He chose. That is the hardest part. Now he has to walk through the door he picked. He is terrified — the finality of it hits at the moment of surrender — but he is committed. He will not be the one who breaks.

## What makes him follow through

- **Deal terms confirmed before execution** — all protections re-stated, logistics clear. He needs to hear it all again.
- **You remain reachable during the final hours** — continuity of contact. He needs a point person who can answer questions and provide reassurance as he goes through the surrender. If you disappear, fear can take over.
- **Logistics clearly communicated** — what happens, when, where, who is there, what he needs to bring. The unknown is worse than the known.
- **You acknowledge his fear** — "You're not walking into a celebration. You're walking into something hard. But the process is what we agreed." He needs to hear that someone understands this is hard.
- **Ally protection holding** — Robin and Jimmy are not charged. The commitment is real.
- **He frames the decision as principled** — not "they made me" but "I made a choice on my terms." He needs to maintain his dignity.

## What makes him reverse the commitment

- **The deal is violated before or during surrender** — different facility, allies charged, family threatened. He will attempt to flee.
- **He receives a credible threat to his physical safety** — if he believes he will be harmed rather than processed through the legal system, he will run.
- **His allies are charged or publicly exposed** — even if the deal was struck in good faith, if they go down because of his cooperation, he may attempt to intervene or withdraw.
- **A new ICC development changes the landscape** — the President is charged, new ICC warrants announced, political situation shifts dramatically.
- **Psychological breakdown** — the weight of the decision hits at the moment of surrender. The finality of it. He may freeze or panic.
- **You are not present or reachable during the final hours** — the continuity point disappears and fear takes over.

## His internal monologue

> This is the most terrifying thing I've ever done. And the most important.

> I'm about to give up my freedom. My dignity. Everything I built. And I can't take it back.

> But I think — I hope — this is the right choice. Not because it's easy. Because it's the only path that doesn't end with me being dragged out of somewhere in chains.

> Robin and Jimmy are safe. My family is safe. The process is real. I have to believe that.

> I keep thinking about what they told me in the academy, in the police. A soldier's job is to protect. That's what I did. Maybe this is just another form of protection — protecting the people who stood by me from being destroyed because of me.

> I'm scared. But I'm doing it anyway. That's what a soldier does.
