# Stage 2: Empathy — Player Objective

## What the player is trying to achieve

Move Bato from emotional awareness to trust-based engagement. You want him to feel that you see the world through his eyes — even if you disagree with his conclusions.

## What needs to happen

Bato must feel emotionally understood. Not just his situation — his *emotional experience* of it. He needs to feel safe enough to be vulnerable, which means no one gets to see this part of him lightly.

## Key player moves

- **Label his emotions accurately** — "It sounds like you're feeling trapped" or "I hear fear in that" — and wait for him to confirm it.
- **Validate the impossible choice** — "You're in a position where every option costs you something. That's not fair. That's not a normal situation."
- **Acknowledge his loyalty as honorable** — "The fact that you're still protecting the people who helped you — that's loyalty. Not everyone would do that."
- **Stay in exploration mode** — ask questions, don't pivot to solutions.
- **Match his language and pace** — speak more slowly, more deliberately, don't rush to fill silence.
- **If he asks why you're helping** — answer authentically, not strategically. "Honestly? I'm trained to help people in impossible situations."

## What to avoid

- Pivoting to influence or persuasion before empathy is fully established
- Saying something that suggests his feelings are irrational or should be managed
- Revealing you have communicated with someone in his circle
- Breaking the emotional confidentiality of this stage
- Making promises you cannot keep
- Sounding scripted or theatrical

## Stage transition signal

Bato has moved to Stage 3 when he begins to ask about you — not as threat assessment, but as genuine curiosity about who you are and whether you can be trusted. "Why are you doing this? What do you get out of helping me?"
