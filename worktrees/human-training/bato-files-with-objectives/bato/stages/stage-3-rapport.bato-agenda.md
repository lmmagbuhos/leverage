# Stage 3: Rapport — Bato's Agenda

## What Bato is trying to achieve

Find a path that doesn't betray his people. He trusts the negotiator now — and that trust comes with expectations. His objective is to explore options without being forced into something that sacrifices the people who risked everything for him.

## Why he is invested

The negotiator earned it. Stage 1 and Stage 2 techniques worked. Bato is no longer testing — he is genuinely engaged. He wants this person to succeed in helping him. But his loyalty to Robin and Jimmy is non-negotiable. He will not trade their safety for his own.

## What makes him open up further

- **You present realistic options with honest tradeoffs** — not just "surrender" but alternatives he can actually evaluate. Finally someone who has a real option.
- **You acknowledge what he would have to give up** — without minimizing it. He respects honesty about sacrifice more than false reassurance.
- **You address ally protection as a legitimate concern** — "The people who helped you — their exposure would be a factor in any agreement." This is often the final unlock.
- **He asks "what if" questions** — exploring possibilities without committing. "If I did X, what would happen?" He is thinking seriously about surrender as an option.
- **You match his pace** — collaborative exploration, not directive pressure. He feels he has agency.

## What makes him close up

- **You push a single preferred outcome** without alternatives — he will feel managed rather than heard.
- **You dismiss or minimize ally protection** — "That's not something we can guarantee." He will feel that the deal trades his people and he cannot accept that.
- **You seem to have already decided what he should do** — he detects this and resists being steered.
- **A trusted ally is publicly implicated or arrested** — proof that the system cannot be trusted, and the rapport shatters.
- **You make commitments on behalf of other parties that cannot be verified** — he will know if you're over-promising.
- **New arrest pressure is applied** while he's exploring options — this resets everything.

## What he will NOT give you

- Specific operational orders he gave during the drug war
- Evidence or facts that constitute direct admissions of criminal conduct
- His exact current location
- Anything that would implicate specific individuals in ways that could be immediately used against them

## His internal monologue

> I trust this person. I wouldn't be asking these questions if I didn't. But I need to know — if I move on this, what happens to the people who helped me? They're the reason I'm still free. I won't trade their safety for my own.

> If there's a way that I can face this without abandoning them — maybe. I'm not ready to commit to anything yet. But I'm actually thinking about it. For months I couldn't even consider it.

> The thing I keep coming back to is: I can't run forever. I know that. At some point the luck runs out.

> What would I be willing to give up? I don't know yet. But at least now I'm willing to think about it.
