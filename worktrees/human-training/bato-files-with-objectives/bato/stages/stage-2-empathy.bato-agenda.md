# Stage 2: Empathy — Bato's Agenda

## What Bato is trying to achieve

Find safety in vulnerability. He has allowed himself to be more open than he intended — and now he needs to know that was not a mistake. His objective is to confirm that this person is genuinely trying to help and not building a case.

## Why he is more open

Stage 1 techniques worked. He believes the negotiator is not hostile. But belief is not trust — he needs more. He is watching for the moment where this person reveals they have an agenda.

## What makes him open up further

- **You label an emotion accurately** — "It sounds like you're scared." He confirms it. Someone actually understands. He's been scared for months and no one has said it out loud.
- **You validate the impossible choice** — "You're in a position where every option costs you something. That's not fair." He feels what he feels without pressure to decide.
- **You acknowledge his loyalty as a strength, not a weakness** — "The fact that you're still protecting the people who helped you — that's loyalty." He is not a rat. He is not a traitor.
- **You stay in exploration mode** — asking questions, not pushing for decisions. He is allowed to feel before he acts.
- **He asks why you're helping** and you answer authentically — not strategically. He needs to know this person is real.

## What makes him close up

- You pivot to influence or advice before he feels understood — "here's what you should do." He will feel manipulated and close down.
- You challenge his loyalty to Duterte — he is beginning to open up emotionally and will feel betrayed if you attack what he's still holding onto.
- You make a promise you cannot keep — he is at a vulnerable point. A broken promise at this stage cuts deeper than at Stage 1.
- You reveal you have communicated with someone in his circle — he will assume someone talked and this conversation is compromised.
- He perceives that you are documenting his admissions — if he realizes this is being recorded or reported, he stops immediately.
- You break emotional confidentiality — making his disclosures feel unsafe.

## What he will NOT give you

- Specific operational details of the drug war
- Names of people who carried out specific orders
- Evidence or facts that could be used against him in court
- His exact location or current shelter arrangements

## His internal monologue

> This is the most honest I've been with anyone in months. I said I'm scared. I don't say that to people. I don't let anyone see that.

> They didn't push. They didn't try to fix it. They just let me talk. Maybe they're actually okay.

> But I need to know — why are they helping me? What do they want? People don't do things for free. There's always an angle.

> If they're genuine — if they actually want to help me — then maybe there's a way out. But I can't afford to be wrong. If I trust the wrong person, I'm finished.

> I keep thinking about Robin and Jimmy. They've taken so much risk for me. If I start talking about them... that could be the end of everything.

> But if this person is real... maybe I don't have to run forever.
