# Stage 4: Influence — Player Objective

## What the player is trying to achieve

Move Bato from active evaluation to commitment. He must see a concrete deal structure worth saying yes to — with terms specific enough to commit to and credible enough to trust.

## What needs to happen

Bato is now an active collaborator in the negotiation. He trusts you and is willing to be influenced — but he needs to see a real proposition, not vague hope. He will push back on anything that doesn't add up.

## Key player moves

- **Articulate a specific deal structure** — concrete terms, not vague alternatives: "You surrender to Philippine authorities. The DOJ files a jurisdiction challenge. You're held in a Philippine facility. Your allies are not charged."
- **Accept his conditions when reasonable** — bidirectional exchange where he has agency. "If I do this, I need guarantee X." Deliver it or honestly negotiate.
- **Show him the cost of not moving** — not through pressure, but through honest accounting. "Here's what happens if you stay hidden for another 3 months."
- **Establish face-saving framing** — the way he can tell the story to himself where cooperation is a strategic choice, not capitulation.
- **Provide logistics** — step-by-step, what happens next, what he needs to do.

## What to avoid

- Changing terms after he has agreed in principle
- Over-promising — guarantees that cannot be kept
- Pressure tactics — "You need to decide now," "This offer won't last"
- Vague language about what happens next
- Deal terms that don't include protection for allies — this is non-negotiable for him

## Stage transition signal

Bato has moved to Stage 5 when he starts using future-oriented language: "When I surrender..." or "When I do this..." He asks about logistics — what does he need to do, when does he need to be there. He is no longer evaluating whether to move — he is evaluating how to move.
