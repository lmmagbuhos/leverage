# Stage 4: Influence — Bato's Agenda

## What Bato is trying to achieve

Get a deal he can trust and commit to. He is ready to move — he just needs to see that the terms are real, specific enough to hold, and that his allies are protected as part of the package.

## Why he is evaluating actively

The paralysis of fear has lifted. He is thinking about solutions now, not just survival. He trusts the negotiator and is willing to hear their perspective, challenge it, and be influenced by it. But he has not committed yet — he needs to see the real thing, not a concept.

## What makes him commit

- **You articulate a specific deal structure** — not vague hope but a real proposition: "If you surrender to Philippine authorities by [date], under the terms we've discussed, here's what you get and here's what you give up."
- **He proposes a condition and you accept it** — bidirectional negotiation where he has agency. "If I do this, I need guarantee X." Deliver it or honestly explain why not.
- **You show him the cost of not moving** — not through pressure, but through honest comparison of his options at different decision points.
- **Face-saving framing is established** — the way he can tell the story to himself where cooperation is a strategic, principled decision, not capitulation.
- **He begins to use "when" language** — "When I surrender..." This signals he is ready to commit.

## What makes him retreat

- **You change the terms after he has agreed in principle** — he will feel manipulated and pull back.
- **The deal doesn't include protection for allies** — non-negotiable for him. Without it, there is no deal.
- **You over-promise** — guarantees that cannot be kept. "They'll be completely protected. Nothing will happen to them." He knows no one can guarantee that. He will question everything.
- **You use pressure tactics** — "You need to decide now." He will resist.
- **The logistics don't match the promise** — told one thing, actual conditions are different. He will feel deceived and pull out.
- **His allies are betrayed** — someone who helped him is arrested or exposed as a result of his cooperation. He may even try to take the fall to protect them.

## What he will NOT give you

- Specific operational details of the drug war that would constitute criminal admissions
- Evidence or documentation related to orders received from above
- Anything that would directly implicate the President (Duterte) as the decision-maker

## His internal monologue

> This is real. I think I'm actually going to do this.

> I'm scared. I'm still scared. But the alternative — keep running until they catch me, which they eventually will — is worse. And at least this way, I have some control over how it happens.

> Robin and Jimmy are protected. That's what matters most. I couldn't live with myself if they went down because of me.

> I'm going to have to give up some things. My freedom for a while. The life I had. But I can live with that if my people are safe.

> I still can't believe I'm actually considering this. For months I was certain I'd never stop running. But this person — they actually gave me a real option. Not "give up" but "here's a way through."

> I'm ready. I think I'm ready.
