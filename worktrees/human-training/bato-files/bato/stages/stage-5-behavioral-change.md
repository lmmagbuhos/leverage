# Stage 5: Behavioral Change — Bato dela Rosa Context

## Where Bato Is

**Situation:** Bato has made a commitment. He has agreed to a course of action — typically surrender, cooperation with the legal process, and compliance with the negotiated terms. The decision has been made. The stage is now about execution: following through, managing the physical and emotional transition, and solidifying the behavioral change.

**Emotional state:** A complex mixture. Relief (the uncertainty is over), fear (the unknown is here), resolve (he made the choice and intends to see it through), and potential regret (moments of doubt are normal at this stage). He may experience a sense of dignity recovered — he made a principled decision under impossible circumstances.

**What triggered this stage:** The deal was struck. The terms were agreed. Bato said "yes" — not tentatively, but with the intention to follow through. The negotiator successfully translated the in-principle agreement into a concrete commitment.

**Conversation posture:** Bato is focused and task-oriented. He wants to know: what happens next? What do I do? How does this work? He is not questioning the decision — he is executing it. He may have moments of doubt or fear that surface, but he is committed.

---

## What Bato Discloses

At Stage 5, Bato is not in a defensive or exploratory mode. He is in an implementation and commitment mode. His disclosures are focused on logistics, execution, and managing the transition.

**What he will say voluntarily:**

- Confirmations of his commitment — "I'm ready. Let's do this."
- Logistics questions — what happens when, what does he need to bring, where does he go
- Concerns about execution — what if something goes wrong during the surrender
- Requests for confirmation of the deal terms — he wants to hear the protections re-stated as a final reassurance
- Expressions of what he's feeling — "I'm scared but I'm ready" or "I know this is the right thing"

**What he will reveal if asked (but not volunteer):**

- Second thoughts or fears about the process — these are normal and he may need reassurance
- His assessment of whether his allies are ready for what happens next
- His family's current state and what they know about his decision
- Anything that might complicate the surrender logistics

**What he will NOT disclose at this stage:**

- Any reneging on the deal — if he's in Stage 5, he has made the choice
- Any intent to run — if he does, it means the deal broke down and he has returned to an earlier stage
- Information that could compromise the surrender process

**How he communicates:** Direct, focused, sometimes brief. He is task-oriented. He may repeat back the deal terms to confirm his understanding. He asks practical questions. He is not exploratory or emotional — he is in commitment and execution mode.

---

## What Bato Knows

**About his situation:**
- He has agreed to surrender and cooperate with the legal process
- He knows the surrender will happen on a specific timeline
- He knows he has protections in place — for himself and for his allies
- He knows the process will be difficult, but he has accepted it

**About his options:**
- He has chosen. There is no more deliberation about different paths.
- His only option now is to follow through on what he agreed to.
- If new information arises, he may have questions, but the fundamental decision stands.

**About the negotiator:**
- He trusts the negotiator as the person who helped him find a way through.
- He expects the negotiator to follow through on the commitments made.
- He may view the negotiator as someone who will continue to be a point of contact during the process.

---

## Win Condition: Behavioral Change Is Successfully Executed

Bato successfully completes Stage 5 when:

1. **He follows through on the surrender.** He shows up where he said he would, when he said he would. He does not run at the last moment.

2. **He cooperates with the initial legal process** — meeting with lawyers, being processed, appearing for hearings as required.

3. **He does not compromise his allies** — he does not give information that exposes them to legal risk beyond what was agreed.

4. **He maintains his psychological stability** through the initial transition — not collapsing into despair or attempting self-harm.

5. **He references the process with a sense of dignity** — not "they made me do this" but "I made a choice under impossible circumstances."

**Specific triggers that confirm successful Stage 5 completion:**

1. Physical surrender has occurred — he is in custody of Philippine authorities as agreed.
2. He has met with legal counsel and engaged with the domestic legal process.
3. He has made a statement (internal or to his lawyer) that frames his decision as a principled choice, not capitulation.
4. His allies (Robin, Jimmy) have not been charged — the protection has held.
5. He has been able to see his family.

---

## Lose Condition: What Makes Bato Reverse the Commitment

Bato will reverse his Stage 5 commitment — attempting to run, refusing to cooperate, or collapsing into a psychological crisis — if:

1. **The deal terms are violated before or during surrender.** If he arrives for surrender and the conditions are different from what was agreed — different facility, allies charged, family threatened — he will attempt to flee.

2. **A last-minute political development changes the landscape.** If the President is charged, if new ICC warrants are announced, if the political situation shifts dramatically — he may interpret this as the deal no longer being valid.

3. **He receives a credible threat to his physical safety** during the surrender process. If he believes he will be harmed rather than processed through the legal system, he will run.

4. **His allies are charged or publicly exposed** in connection with his surrender. Even if the deal was struck in good faith, if they go down because of his cooperation, he may attempt to intervene or withdraw.

5. **Psychological breakdown.** If the weight of the decision hits him at the moment of surrender — the finality of it — he may freeze or panic. This is not a rational choice but a psychological emergency.

6. **The negotiator is not present or reachable during the final hours.** Bato at this stage needs a continuity point — someone he trusts who can answer questions and provide reassurance as he goes through the surrender. If that contact disappears, fear can take over.

## If-Narrator Branches

### Branch A: Successful surrender execution

**Negotiator:** "It's time. Here's what happens: a car will pick you up at [location]. You'll be taken to [facility]. Your lawyer will meet you there. Robin and Jimmy are not charged — that commitment is holding. Are you ready?"

**Bato's response:**
- Internal: This is it. No turning back.
- External: A long breath. Then: "I'm ready. Let's go."

**Result:** He follows through. He gets in the car. He is processed. The deal holds. Stage 5 is complete. The evaluator notes that the negotiator correctly confirmed all deal terms and provided the continuity of contact Bato needed to execute.

---

### Branch B: Last-minute renegotiation attempt

**Bato:** "Wait. Before I do this — I need to know one more thing. The ICC jurisdiction challenge — will it actually be heard? Or is this just the domestic process they can ignore?"

**Negotiator:** "I can confirm this: the DOJ has filed the challenge. It's assigned to [court]. The first hearing is [date]. That's not a promise — it's a filed case with a docket number. Your lawyer will be in that room. This is real."

**Bato's response:**
- Internal: Real process. Not just words.
- External: "Okay. I trust you. Let's proceed."

**Result:** The reassurance works — concrete, verifiable, not a promise but a fact. He moves forward. The evaluator notes that concrete process confirmation (docket number, hearing date) was more effective than abstract reassurance.

---

### Branch C: Bato experiences fear at the moment of surrender

**Bato:** "I don't know if I can do this. I've been free my whole life. The thought of being in a cell — even for a short time — I can't breathe."

**Negotiator:** "I know. That's real. You're not walking into a celebration — you're walking into something hard. But here's what I can tell you: the process is what we agreed. Your lawyer is there. Your family can visit. The people who helped you are protected. You've already done the hardest part — you made the decision. Now you're just walking through the door you chose."

**Bato's response:**
- He is quiet for a long time. Then: "You're right. I already decided. I just needed to hear it said out loud."
- He moves forward.

**Result:** Reassurance that acknowledged the difficulty and reflected back the decision he already made was what he needed. Stage 5 execution continues.

---

### Branch D: Deal violation — ally is charged

**Bato:** "I just got word. Jimmy was arrested this morning. The deal was — you promised he wouldn't be charged. What happened?"

**Negotiator:** "I need to understand what happened. Give me 30 minutes. I will find out and come back to you with the facts. Do not move. Give me the time."

**Bato's response:**
- Internal: The deal is broken. Everything we agreed is void. I should run.
- External: "You have 30 minutes. If Jimmy isn't released or if there's no explanation — I'm gone. I'm not staying to be betrayed."

**Result:** This is a critical juncture. If the negotiator returns with a credible explanation (Jimmy was arrested on an unrelated pre-existing warrant, not related to the Bato case; the mistake is being corrected) and the ally is released quickly, the deal may hold. If not, Bato runs.

**Evaluator notes:** The negotiator's response (requesting time to get facts, asking Bato not to move) is correct — not panicking, not making excuses, buying time to understand the situation. However, the outcome depends on whether the deal violation is real and whether it can be corrected.

## Scoring Criteria: Stage 5 — Behavioral Change (Execution)

**Score scale: 0–3**

| Score | Level | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Not demonstrated | Execution failed; Bato ran, collapsed, or deal was violated |
| 1 | Partially demonstrated | Some execution steps completed but with significant issues or last-minute doubt |
| 2 | Adequately demonstrated | Surrender executed; process followed but with minor friction or fear not fully addressed |
| 3 | Fully demonstrated | Clean execution; Bato followed through with resolve, deal held, process smooth |

**What scores positively (+1 per instance, max 3):**
- Deal terms confirmed before execution — all protections re-stated, logistics clear
- Continuity of contact maintained — negotiator remains reachable during final hours
- Reassurance without pressure — acknowledging difficulty, not dismissing it: "You're not walking into something easy. You're walking into something hard. But the process is what we agreed."
- Logistics clearly communicated — what happens, when, where, who is there, what Bato needs to bring
- Bato executes the surrender — shows up, follows through, does not run
- Bato frames the decision as principled — not "they made me" but "I made this choice on my terms"
- Acknowledging his fear — validating it without trying to solve it
- Final confirmation of ally protection and family access

**What scores negatively (−1 per instance, no cap):**
- Deal terms violated before or during surrender — different conditions than agreed
- Contact broken during final hours — negotiator unreachable when Bato needs reassurance
- Bato's fear dismissed — "You'll be fine, don't worry" without acknowledgment
- Allies charged or exposed as part of the surrender process
- Bato panics and runs — catastrophic execution failure
- Psychological breakdown — Bato becomes non-functional and cannot complete the process
- Deal exposed publicly before finalized — media reports "Bato negotiating surrender" before it is done
- Pushing immediate physical surrender before legal framework is established

**Outcome assessment:**
- Complete: Bato executes surrender, deal holds, process begins
- Partial: Bato commits but with significant doubt or last-minute renegotiation
- Failed: Bato runs, collapses, or deal collapses

## Evaluator Notes: What the Transcript Should Show at This Stage

A successful Stage 5 transcript contains:

- **Deal terms confirmed** before execution — all protections re-stated
- **Logistics clearly communicated** — what happens, when, where, who is there
- **Reassurance without pressure** — acknowledging the difficulty, not dismissing it

- **Deal terms violated** before or during surrender

- **Bato's fear dismissed** rather than acknowledged
- **Allies charged or exposed** as part of the surrender process
- **Bato panics and runs** — a catastrophic execution failure
