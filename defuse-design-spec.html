02

FBI Behavioral Change Stairway

Developed by the FBI's Crisis Negotiation Unit. Five stages, each building on the last. Moving too fast breaks trust — and trust is everything. This is the game's progression system.

Key design insight: Every stage has a voice signature. Stage 1 requires you to slow down and listen. Stage 3 requires warmth in tone. Claude doesn't just evaluate words — it evaluates whether your vocal delivery matches the technique required for that stage.
01
Stage One
Active Listening

Don't talk too much. Mirror their words back. Let them vent. Prove you're present before you try to solve anything. The FBI found this — more than any tactic — is what lowers the initial temperature.

Marcus: Panicked Technique: Mirroring Win: Suspect slows breathing Fail: Player interrupts or demands
02
Stage Two
Empathy

Name their emotion out loud — without judging it. "You feel cornered. Like nobody listened until now." Emotion labeling is one of the most powerful tools in negotiation. Don't comment on whether the feelings are valid.

Marcus: Angry Technique: Emotion Labeling Win: Shoulders visibly drop Fail: Player argues or minimizes
03
Stage Three
Rapport

Find common ground. Use their name. Slow your voice down deliberately. Buy time — lowering emotion proves more effective than quid pro quo bargaining. A small personal disclosure from them is the win signal.

Marcus: Guarded Technique: Pacing & Mirroring Win: Suspect makes personal disclosure Fail: Player pushes for outcomes
04
Stage Four
Influence

Gently introduce alternatives. "What would it look like if this ended differently?" Emotional connection from Stage 3 makes influence possible. Nearly every successful release happens here — after connection, not before.

Marcus: Conflicted Technique: Open Questions Win: "What happens if I let them go?" Fail: Player makes ultimatums
05
Stage Five
Behavioral Change

Get them to say "that's right" — not "you're right." Don't rush. Don't claim victory. Don't celebrate early. Silence is your most powerful tool at this stage. Patience wins.

Marcus: Surrendering Technique: Patience + Silence Win: Hostage released ✓ Fail: Player rushes the moment

Agora Features & Architecture

Every Agora feature used is load-bearing. Remove any one of them and the experience degrades meaningfully.

On tone & emotion detection: Agora ConvoAI natively supports real-time voice processing and emotional AI — confirmed in Agora's ConvoAI Device Kit powering the Fuzozo companion robot. For technique scoring beyond native emotion, we layer Claude's sentiment analysis on the live transcript stream. Agora handles real-time audio transport. Claude reads emotional subtext. Both are essential and distinct.

Full Technology Stack

Every library chosen for a specific reason. No unnecessary dependencies. GSAP is not needed — TalkingHead manages its own animation loop and all UI meters use native CSS transitions.

Avatar: TalkingHead.js

TalkingHead is a pure browser JavaScript class that handles everything — 3D rendering, lip sync, facial expressions, and body animations. No React, no Three Fiber, no separate animation library needed.

What TalkingHead Handles Natively
  • ✓3D rendering via Three.js (internal)
  • ✓Lip sync from audio stream or TTS
  • ✓ARKit + Oculus blendshape expressions
  • ✓Emoji → facial expression conversion
  • ✓Mixamo FBX body animations
  • ✓Eye blinking, gaze, head movement
  • ✓Smooth blendshape transitions via setFixedValue()
  • ✓Multiple avatar support (Marcus + others)
Avatar Source: VRoid Studio

Free desktop app. Create Marcus in 30 minutes — adjust face shape, skin tone, clothing, expression. Export as GLB. TalkingHead loads it directly. No API keys, no accounts, works offline.

Free & offline GLB export Stylized aesthetic ARKit compatible

The stylized anime aesthetic reads as "intentional game character" rather than uncanny valley. This is a feature, not a compromise.

07

Game Mechanics

Four interlocking systems create pressure from different directions. Each is driven by a different input source.

🌡️ Tension Meter

Visible red bar at all times. Goes up when: player demands, threatens, interrupts, or goes silent too long (VAD). Goes down when: player mirrors, empathizes, names emotions. Three spikes to max = Crisis Event. Three crisis events = Game Over.

Driver: VAD + Claude CSS transition
📺 Public Pressure Meter

Driven by Isabel's live reporting. Rises steadily from Stage 3 onward. High pressure reduces Rivera's patience timer. Player cannot directly control this — only indirectly by advancing stages faster.

Driver: Timer + Stage CSS transition
🪜 Stage Progress

Does not advance on time. Advances only when Claude detects the correct FBI technique used in the right context. Stage 4 cannot be reached without Stage 3's win condition. No shortcuts.

Driver: Claude only
📻 Rivera Interrupt

Every 90 seconds, Rivera's radio crackles with conflicting instructions. Player has 15 seconds to respond or ignore. Following his aggressive advice raises Marcus's tension. Ignoring too long triggers SWAT threat.

Driver: Timer Agora RTC channel

Demo Mode

Demo mode is not optional. A clearly visible button row — S1 → S2 → S3 → S4 → S5 + CRISIS + WIN + FAIL — allows jumping to any stage with Marcus pre-loaded at the correct emotional state. The skip is intentionally visible. It's part of the pitch narrative, not something to hide.

The Pitch (30s)

"This is DEFUSE. You're a crisis negotiator. One hostage. BGC Bank. No weapons, no health bars. Just your voice — and the FBI's actual de-escalation framework used in real hostage situations." Show the full UI. Let them take it in.

30 seconds. Sets stakes. FBI mention earns instant credibility.

0:30–1:00

Stage 1 — Show a good response (30s)

Team member speaks calmly. Marcus shifts from Panicked → Agitated (progress). Tension drops slightly. Say: "The AI isn't scoring your words. It's scoring how you speak. Pace. Tone. Empathy signal."

Establish the core mechanic early so the judge understands what they're watching.

1:00–1:30

Stage 2 — Show failure deliberately (30s)

Jump to Stage 2. Make a demanding statement. Tension spikes. Marcus goes Angry. Rivera crackles in with a radio interrupt. ElevenLabs police radio audio fires. Say: "That's what a closed question does in Stage 2. Rivera isn't helping."

Showing failure is more impressive than showing success. Proves real evaluation — not keyword matching.

1:30–2:30

Stage 3 — Live recovery (60s)

Recover from the failure using correct empathy labeling. Show tension meter fall in real time via CSS transition. Isabel ticker updates: "Negotiator appears to be gaining ground." Say: "Watch the face. Watch the meter. That's Agora's transcript stream being analyzed by Claude in real time."

60 seconds here is enough to show the full recovery arc and demonstrate all three meters responding independently.

2:30–3:30

Hand mic to judge — Stage 4 (60s) ← The moment

"You're at Stage 4. He's considering surrender. One wrong word and he escalates. You have 60 seconds. Don't threaten. Don't demand. Ask him what a different ending looks like." Hand mic. Say nothing. Let it run fully.

The judge's face reacting live IS the product demonstrating itself. Whatever happens is the demo. This is the only moment that matters.

3:30–4:00

Show both endings (30s)

WIN (15s): Marcus surrenders, hands up, ticker: "PEACEFULLY RESOLVED." ElevenLabs: crowd exhale, radio chatter clears. FAIL (15s): Red flash, SWAT breach, ticker escalates. ElevenLabs: tactical breach sound, chaos audio.

Both endings in 30 seconds shows full game scope without playing the full game.

4:00–4:30

Scorecard reveal (30s)

Show the post-game training report. "This is the product under the game. FBI technique scores, AI coach feedback, a session report you can share with your team or supervisor." Let judges read it for a moment.

The scorecard reframes everything from game to product. This is where the pitch lands.

4:30–5:00

Close (30s)

"Built with Agora ConvoAI and RTC SDK, TalkingHead.js, ElevenLabs for spatial audio, TRAE IDE, and Claude for real-time FBI technique scoring. Crisis negotiation training costs thousands per session with a human facilitator. DEFUSE makes it something people actually want to do — and do again."

Closes with full tech stack callout (Agora judges), training product frame, and a repeatable hook.

11

2-Minute Q&A Prep

Six questions judges will ask. Manager owns the answers. Practice these out loud before the demo — not just reading them.

Rule for Q&A: Every answer must be under 20 seconds. If you go longer, you're rambling. One sentence setup, one sentence answer, stop.
Q: "Why does this need voice? Couldn't you just type?"

"Typing removes the entire skill being trained. A calm tone, a deliberate pace, a warm delivery — you can't fake those in text. The AI is grading your voice, not your vocabulary."

Q: "How does the AI know if I used the right technique?"

"Agora transcribes speech in real time. Claude reads the transcript against a system prompt defining each FBI technique by linguistic pattern — mirroring repeats key phrases, emotion labeling names feelings explicitly. It returns a technique ID and score delta on every turn."

Q: "Is this just a chatbot with a face?"

"No — a chatbot with a face responds to content. DEFUSE responds to delivery. Say the right words in the wrong tone and the tension still rises. That's the difference."

Q: "Who actually uses this?"

"Law enforcement de-escalation training is mandated in most countries post-2020. Social workers, HR professionals, crisis hotline volunteers — anyone who needs to practice high-stakes conversations without a human actor on the other side."

Q: "What's the Agora-specific innovation?"

"Three Agora features working together that haven't been combined this way: ConvoAI for the character's real-time voice, VAD to detect silence as a game mechanic, and Live Transcripts feeding Claude for scoring. Remove any one and the experience breaks."

Q: "Could you scale this?"

"Yes. Agora handles thousands of simultaneous sessions natively. The scenario is a JSON config — swap the character, context, and stakes. Same engine: medical triage, HR mediation, domestic intervention. Different scenario."
